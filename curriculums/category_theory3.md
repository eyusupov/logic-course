## 📅 PHASE 5: Advanced Postgraduate Module — Category Theory and Program Semantics (Weeks 17–20)

Core Objective: Unify your entire logic education through Category Theory, analyzing exactly how software behavior, data types, and modular designs are formalized and verified algebraically. [1]

## 🔹 Week 17: Categorical Foundations & Denotational Basics

-
- Required Reading: [M&A] Chapter 1 (An Introduction to Denotational Semantics) & Chapter 2 (An Introduction to Category Theory). [2]
- Core Concepts: Structural definition of Categories, Functors, and Natural Transformations; Morphisms, objects, and universal products; Modeling a program's semantics as a mathematical function mapping input data states to output states. [1, 2]
- Why it matters: This builds the formal geometric vocabulary needed to treat programming loops and data blocks as precise algebraic objects. [1]

## 🔹 Week 18: Assertion Semantics, Predicate Transformers, and Proofs

-
- Required Reading: [M&A] Chapter 3 (Partially Additive Semantics) & Chapter 4 (Assertion Semantics).
- Core Concepts: Hoare Logic axioms; Mapping preconditions and postconditions as structural invariants across software blocks; Building logical verification proofs that code functions satisfy their explicit specifications.
- Why it matters: This is the exact math used to perform compile-time full program verification on critical safety components. [1, 2]
-

## 🔹 Week 19: Order Semantics and Canonical Fixed Points

-
- Required Reading: [M&A] Chapter 5 (Recursive Specifications), Chapter 6 (Order Semantics of Recursion), & Chapter 7 (Canonical Fixed Points). [2]
- Core Concepts: Complete Partially Ordered sets (CPOs); Scott-Strachey Denotational Semantics; Solving structural software recursion by calculating continuous Least Fixed-Points on functional domains. [1, 2]
- Why it matters: You connect the fixed-point math you learned in Week 16 for database queries to the structural evaluation of software loops and recursive function states. [1]

## 🔹 Week 20: Equational & Parametric Specification of Abstract Data Types (ADTs)

-
- Required Reading: [M&A] Chapter 10 (Recursive Specification of Data Types), Chapter 11 (Parametric Specification), & Chapter 13 (Equational Specification).
