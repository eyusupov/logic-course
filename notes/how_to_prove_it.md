Yes, Daniel J. Velleman's [How to Prove It: A Structured Approach](https://www.google.com/search?q=how+to+prove+it:+a+structured+approach&kgmid=/g/12by31xd2) is a fantastic addition, but only if used as a mandatory "Zero-Week" pre-requisite or concurrent night-table reading during Phase 1.
If you try to slot it directly into the 16-week grid as an equal textbook alongside Enderton and Harrison, it will slow down your momentum. Velleman teaches the practical, informal English-and-math strategies that human mathematicians use to write proofs. Enderton and Harrison teach the rigid, automated mathematical structures that machines use to process logic.
Here is exactly how Velleman solves a hidden vulnerability in your curriculum, why it fits Phase 1, and how to use it strategically.

---

## 🔍 The Problem Velleman Solves

As your repo critic sharply pointed out: "Week 1 assumes comfort with formal proof/induction with zero stated prerequisite."
When Enderton says "Prove by structural induction on the generation of well-formed formulas that..." in Week 2, he assumes you already know how to set up a math proof. If your proof-writing foundations are rusty, you will get completely stuck. Velleman bridges this exact gap. He teaches you how to think like a logician before you attempt to build logic systems.

## 📅 Where to Map Velleman to Phase 1 (Weeks 1–3)

Do not assign whole chapters of Velleman. Instead, use his book as a targeted Proof Mechanics Toolkit to help you survive Enderton's toughest initial sections:

## 🔹 During Week 1 (Syntax Definitions)

-
- Read: [VEL] Chapter 1 & 2 (Sentential Logic / Quantifiers).
- The Benefit: Velleman translates standard logical operators ($\to, \leftrightarrow, \neg$) and binders (∀, ∃) into clear conversational strategies. It ensures you know how to read mathematical notation without translating it awkwardly in your head.
-

## 🔹 During Week 2 (The Truth & Parsing Swap)

-
- Read: [VEL] Chapter 3 (Proofs) & Chapter 6 (Induction).
- The Benefit: This is the most critical intersection. Enderton Chapter 1.4 introduces Structural Induction. Velleman Chapter 6 unpacks standard Mathematical Induction and Strong Induction step-by-step. Reading Velleman gives you the mental blueprint for a standard induction proof (Base Case, Inductive Hypothesis, Inductive Step), so you can immediately apply it to Enderton’s complex string-parsing trees.
-

## 🔹 During Week 3 (Propositional Math & Normal Forms)

-
- Read: [VEL] Chapter 4 (Relations) & Chapter 5 (Functions).
- The Benefit: When Enderton introduces a Truth Assignment as a formal mathematical function mapping the set of sentences to the set $\{0, 1\}$, Velleman’s chapters ensure you perfectly understand domain mappings, injections, and set-theoretic Cartesian relations.
